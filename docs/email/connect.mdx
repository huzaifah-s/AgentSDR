A mailbox (for example `rep@yourcompany.com`) is an address campaigns send from and replies arrive at. There are two ways to add one, and an organization can mix them:

- **Google Workspace**: a Google Workspace service account sends and reads Gmail for every address on your domain. You connect it once for the organization, then add addresses with no password. See [Connect Google Workspace](#connect-google-workspace).
- **Any other provider (SMTP + IMAP)**: Zoho, Fastmail, Yahoo, iCloud, GoDaddy, cPanel or other hosting, or your company's own mail server. Each mailbox signs in with its own username and (usually app) password: SMTP sends, IMAP reads the replies. Nothing to connect first. See [Add a mailbox](#add-a-mailbox).

All of it lives under **Settings → Email**, which has three tabs: **Mailboxes**, **Connection** and **Sending rules**.

<Info>
Microsoft 365 / Outlook is not supported yet: Microsoft is retiring password sign-in for SMTP and IMAP, so it needs its own connection. It is listed on the **Connection** tab as "coming soon".
</Info>

## Connect Google Workspace

Open **Settings → Email → Connection**. The **Google Workspace** card shows **Connected** or **Not connected**, and what it powers: email accounts, campaigns and reply sync.

<Frame caption="The Connection tab. Connect on the Google Workspace card opens the setup dialog.">
  <img src="/assets/screenshots/app/email-connection.png" alt="Settings, Email, Connection tab showing a Google Workspace card marked Not connected with a Connect button, and a Microsoft Outlook card marked Coming soon" />
</Frame>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Click Connect">
    The card opens a form with a **How to connect** guide. It lists the Google Cloud and Google Admin steps: enable the Gmail API, create a service account and a JSON key, then authorise the service account for the Gmail scopes in the Admin console (domain-wide delegation). The full walkthrough is in [Google Workspace setup](/integrations/google-workspace).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in the credentials">
    Use **Upload key file** to fill the fields from the JSON key, or paste them by hand.

    - **Service account email** (required)
    - **Private key** (required)
    - **Gmail Pub/Sub topic** (optional)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    AgentSDR checks that Google accepts the key before it saves, so a typo fails on the form rather than on your first send. A topic that is filled in must look like `projects/<project>/topics/<topic>`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
Without the **Gmail Pub/Sub topic**, sending works but replies are not picked up. Leave it empty only if you do not need reply detection, because reply detection is what stops a sequence when someone answers.
</Warning>

Once connected, the card shows **Edit**, **Disconnect** and when the credentials were last verified. Disconnect pauses everything that depends on Google: Google mailboxes stop sending and syncing (SMTP + IMAP mailboxes carry on), email pages show a **Connect email** prompt when the organization has no other mailbox, and nothing is read from env variables as a fallback. Owners and admins manage this; see [Organizations](/workspace/organizations).

## Add a mailbox

Go to **Settings → Email → Mailboxes** and click **Add mailbox**. The dialog, **Add a mailbox**, starts with a choice: **Google Workspace** (available once Google is connected) or **Other provider (SMTP + IMAP)**.

### A Google Workspace address

The Google credentials above let AgentSDR act for your domain. A mailbox is one address on that domain.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose Google Workspace">
    Pick **Google Workspace** at the top of the dialog.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the address">

    - **Email address** (required): a Google Workspace address covered by your domain-wide delegation.
    - **Display name** (optional): the name recipients see beside the address.
    - **Signature** (optional): added to every send from this mailbox.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect and test">
    Click **Connect & test**. AgentSDR calls Gmail as that address. If it works you see **Mailbox connected**. If not, **The connection test failed** shows Google's reason and you can click **Try again**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Add a mailbox from another provider (SMTP + IMAP)

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose Other provider (SMTP + IMAP)">
    It is the only choice while Google Workspace is not connected.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the provider">
    **Provider** fills in the servers for Zoho Mail, Fastmail, Gmail (app password), Yahoo Mail, iCloud Mail and GoDaddy Professional Email. For anything else, pick **Other** and type them in; your provider's help pages list them as "SMTP server" and "IMAP server".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the address and login">
    - **Email address** (required): the address campaigns send from.
    - **Username** (required): follows the address, which is right for nearly every provider.
    - **Password** (required): most providers need an **app password** here, not the account's own, especially with two-factor sign-in on. The hint under the field says where to make one for the chosen provider. It is stored encrypted and used only to send from and read this mailbox.
    - **SMTP server**, **IMAP server**, their **Port** and **Security**: port 465 (SMTP) and 993 (IMAP) mean **SSL/TLS**; 587 means **STARTTLS**. Connections are always encrypted: a server that cannot upgrade with STARTTLS is refused rather than sent the password in the clear.
    - **Save a copy of each email to the Sent folder** (on by default): plain SMTP does not keep sent mail, so AgentSDR files a copy in the mailbox's Sent folder over IMAP, and your sends show up in the mailbox's own mail app. Turn it off for a provider that files SMTP sends itself (Gmail does), or each would appear twice.
    - **Display name** and **Signature** (optional), as for Google.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect and test">
    Click **Connect & test**. AgentSDR signs in to both servers without sending anything. If either refuses, **The connection test failed** says which one (sending or replies) and why, and nothing is saved.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Replies to an SMTP + IMAP mailbox are read from its **INBOX every two minutes** (Google mailboxes get theirs within seconds). Reading starts from the moment the mailbox is added, so older mail is not mistaken for replies, and nothing is marked as read. Replies and bounces then go where Google's do: the Inbox, the CRM, and stopping the lead's sequence.

To change the servers or password later, choose **Change login** on the mailbox's **...** menu. Leave the password empty to keep the saved one. The change is tested first and a failed test leaves the mailbox as it was, so a typo cannot stop its campaigns.

<Note>
Some providers cap how many emails a mailbox may send a day over SMTP (shared hosting often allows only a few hundred). Keep the mailbox's daily limit under your provider's.
</Note>

<Note>
For self-hosted AgentSDR: a mail server is refused if its name resolves to a private or internal address (`10.x`, `192.168.x`, `localhost` and similar). If your mail server is on your own network, set `EMAIL_ALLOW_PRIVATE_HOSTS=true`.
</Note>

An address can belong to only one organization. If it is already connected elsewhere, the dialog says so.

## Read the Mailboxes table

Each row shows a status, how many emails went out today against the mailbox's daily limit, and its sending hours.

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Connected** | Can send. |
| **Failed** | The last connection test failed. The error appears under the address; click **Test again**. |

Under each address the table shows how the mailbox connects: **Google Workspace**, or **SMTP + IMAP** with its SMTP server. A problem reading replies (for example a changed password) appears under the address too, while the mailbox keeps its status until a test fails.
| **Connecting** | The first test has not finished. |
| **Disabled** | Not sending. |

Only **Connected** mailboxes send. The cards above the table summarise **Connected**, **Needs attention**, **Sent today** and **Left today** (daily limits of connected mailboxes minus what they sent). Failed mailboxes are sorted to the top.

Use the **...** menu on a row for **Test connection**, **Send test email** (connected mailboxes only), **Change login** (SMTP + IMAP mailboxes), **Sending hours**, **Signature** and **Remove mailbox**. Removing a mailbox stops it sending, and emails scheduled from it do not go out.

## Daily limit

A new mailbox starts with the organization's **Emails per day, per mailbox** rule, which defaults to 30 (allowed 1 to 200). The table shows each mailbox's sent-today count against its own limit, and the queue never schedules more than that limit in a day.

The page warns above about 50 a day from one Google Workspace mailbox, because deliverability usually drops and Google may throttle it. New mailboxes should start low. You change the default under [Sending rules](/workspace/sending-rules).

<Note>
The mailbox table does not currently have a control for editing one mailbox's limit. The limit is copied from the organization rule when the mailbox is added.
</Note>

## Signature

Choose **Signature** on a mailbox to edit it. It is plain text, such as a name and title. In a sequence, put `%signature%` where it should appear; if the email body has no placeholder, the signature is appended at the end. If the mailbox has no signature, the placeholder is removed rather than sent literally.

## Sending hours

Choose **Sending hours** on a mailbox. Set a **Timezone**, then turn on the **Days** you want and a start and end time for each. The mailbox only sends inside this window, and emails due outside it wait for the next open slot. If every day is off, the mailbox will not send at all. A day whose end is before its start is treated as an overnight window.

New mailboxes copy the organization's default: Monday to Friday, 09:00 to 18:00, in `Asia/Kolkata` unless you change it in [Sending rules](/workspace/sending-rules). Changing the default does not rewrite mailboxes that already exist.

Between sends, each mailbox also waits a random gap, 18 to 24 minutes by default. Sending more often than every 10 minutes from one mailbox looks automated to spam filters.
